    In LTE-A or 5G systems, there are two modes of random access
procedure, contention-free and contention-based. In contention-free
mode, preambles are assigned to a UE by the base station. Hence, simul-
taneous transmission of the same preamble by different UEs within a
RAO does not occur. This provides low latency service for services with
high priority, like the cases of handover and positioning. Whereas, in
the contention-based mode, which is used for regular communication
services, a UE randomly selects a preamble from a set of available
preambles through competition among other UEs. In this case, multiple
UEs may select the same preamble in the same RAO, which may lead
to preamble collision, resulting in access failure. However, the number
of possible preamble collisions can be minimized through proper access
control method. This is where the need for our proposed method comes
from.

    The contention-based random access procedure involves the ex-
change of messages between the UE and the base station in a number
of steps equal to the number of messages exchanged. Hence, currently
there are two types of it, namely four-step random access procedure
(included in 3GPP Release 13 and 14) and two-step random access
procedure (included in 3GPP Release 16) [25­27]. For explanation
simplicity and historical precedence, let us first discuss the four-step
random access procedure, followed by the two-step random access
procedure, and finally a remark on our choice among the two.

    The four-step random access procedure involves the exchange of
